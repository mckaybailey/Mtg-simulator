Subject: Lands that become creatures for good: animate them at the end of the opponent's turn
Why: AnimateAi.checkPhaseRestrictions lets every Duration$ Permanent animate through at any time,
  so Great Hall of the Biblioplex ({5}: becomes a 2/4 Wizard creature, permanently; Four-Color
  Control) was animated in the AI's own upkeep (398 times in a 30-games-per-pairing gauntlet of
  Four-Color Control against the meta decks, seed 1, --save-logs), in the opponent's upkeep (123)
  and in its own first main phase (111), and only 12 times in an end step. Mana spent in its own
  upkeep stays tapped for that turn and the opponent's next: on turns 5 and later Four-Color
  Control cast 0.32 spells over the two turns after an upkeep animate, against 1.23 on turns
  without one. Now a land's permanent, instant-speed self-animate with a mana cost is done at the
  opponent's end step, when the mana untaps next anyway, or in the AI's own first main phase or
  beginning of combat when the land is untapped (it can attack) and the mana left afterwards still
  pays for the cheapest instant or flash card in hand; never in an upkeep or earlier on the
  opponent's turn. A first
  version allowed only the opponent's end step and cost Mono-Black Aggro, whose Soulstone
  Sanctuary ({4}: a 3/3 vigilance creature for good) attacks a turn later that way, 2.8 points
  (paired 95% CI -4.0 to -1.5).
